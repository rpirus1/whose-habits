# Everyday habits of Brazil (BR) and Mexico (MX): new candidates and sources for existing rows

Method note: I read CONTENT_RULES.md and rows br-001…br-030 and mx-001…mx-030 first. Every URL below was opened with WebFetch and supported the claim it is attached to, unless the entry says otherwise. Partway through, the session's shared web-search budget ran out (200 searches per turn), so later checks used direct fetches of known pages (mostly Wikipedia). Several existing rows are therefore "none found". That means "not verified in this pass", not "false". Reddit was not reachable through search results, so expat views come from expat.com, Expat Exchange-type blogs, BuzzFeed and Matador interviews, and language blogs.

## A) New candidates

### Takeaway
I found 28 Brazilian and 32 Mexican candidates with opened sources, weighted toward humour, language and work. The strongest BR-only material is idioms (gambiarra, encher linguiça, mão de vaca, fazer a egípcia, sextou) and family rituals (the pavê joke, describe-and-guess Secret Santa, the marriage-teasing birthday verse). The strongest MX-only material is street soundscape (camote whistle, garbage bell, gas jingle), office slang (godín, barbero, chamba) and holiday humour (Día de los Inocentes, Guadalupe-Reyes "marathon").

### Cited Findings: Brazil (BR)

**Humour**
- **Culture:** BR — **Habit:** At Christmas dinner an uncle asks if the layered dessert is "for looking or for eating", every year. — **Detail:** "É pavê ou pá comê?" Pavê sounds like "pra ver" (to see). The teller is called "tio do pavê", meaning a cheesy uncle. A brand turned the joke into a dessert. — **Topic:** humour — **Obviousness:** 3 — **Also fits:** none — **Source:** https://marcaspelomundo.com.br/anunciantes/uniao-lanca-pacume/ — **Confidence:** high (the page calls it "a tradicional e já batida piada")
- **Culture:** BR — **Habit:** In Secret Santa, each giver describes their person with teasing clues while everyone shouts guesses. — **Detail:** At "amigo secreto" or "amigo oculto", the recipient, once guessed, walks up to cheers and then describes the next person. The reveal is the main event. — **Topic:** family — **Obviousness:** 2 — **Also fits:** none (MX "intercambio" exists, but I found no source for the guessing ritual there) — **Source:** https://eatrio.net/2011/12/my-least-favourite-brazilian-christmas-tradition.html — **Confidence:** medium (expat blog, 2011, family setting; offices do it too but this is unverified)
- **Culture:** BR — **Habit:** After the birthday song, guests sing a teasing verse asking whom the birthday person will marry. — **Detail:** "Com quem será?" is a call-and-response that names a crush or partner, to everyone's amusement. — **Topic:** humour — **Obviousness:** 3 — **Also fits:** none — **Source:** https://caminhoslanguages.com/blog/happy-birthday-in-brazil-br — **Confidence:** medium
- **Culture:** BR — **Habit:** Pretending not to have seen someone you'd rather avoid has a name: "doing the Egyptian". — **Detail:** "Fazer a egípcia" means turning your face away and ignoring someone. - **Culture:** BR — **Habit:** A stingy person is said to have "a cow's hand". — **Detail:** "Mão de vaca" means a tightwad. "Pão-duro" (hard bread) means the same thing. — **Topic:** humour — **Obviousness:** 3 — **Also fits:** none (MX says "codo", elbow, which is a different image) — **Source:** https://www.dicio.com.br/maos-de-vaca-2/ — **Confidence:** high
- **Culture:** BR — **Habit:** Being the third wheel on a date is called "holding the candle". — **Detail:** "Segurar vela". — **Topic:** humour — **Obviousness:** 3 — **Also fits:** IT FR (Italian "reggere il moccolo" and French "tenir la chandelle" use the same image; this comes from my own knowledge and was not verified in this pass) — **Source:** https://blog.busuu.com/brazilian-expressions-sound-fluent/ — **Confidence:** high for BR, but the image is shared
- **Culture:** BR — **Habit:** An improvised, held-together-with-tape fix is a national art form with its own word. — **Detail:** "Gambiarra" means a jury-rig or quick fix, and is said with pride and self-mockery. - **Culture:** BR — **Habit:** Lunch is a hot plated meal of at least an hour, often with colleagues. **Humour**
