## A) New candidate statements (PL ≈27, UA ≈27), weighted to humour, language and work

### Takeaway
Language-learning sites and Wikipedia gave plenty of verifiable material on idioms, phrases and school or work rituals, which are the thinnest topics in the current bank. Many idioms are shared by PL and UA, and a few also with FR, so `fits` needs care. The best single-culture picks are PL "no" = yeah, Wąchock jokes, the Sunday Żabka loophole, Boys' Day, studniówka and the academic quarter, and UA "grow big!", the duck curses, the last-bell ceremony and courier-branch pickup.

### Inferences
- PL–UA overlap is heavy for idioms (wolf, pig, white crow, tar in honey) and seasonal folk rituals (Andrzejki/Andriy, Kupala, Easter water). These work only in games without the other culture. For a PL+UA game, the best picks are single-culture items: PL "no" = yeah, Wąchock, Kevin, Boys' Day, studniówka, academic quarter, Żabka loophole; UA duck curses, "grow big!", sarcastic "live well", last bell, Nova Poshta, vyshyvanka day.
- FR is a hidden overlap for several Slavic idioms ("talk of the wolf", squeezed lemon). Add FR to fits when in doubt.
- Off-limits checks: I dropped "mohorych" (a treat for finishing a job, usually alcoholic), the studniówka red-underwear superstition (body/underwear), "O Bozhe" (religion) and "kum/kuma" (baptism-linked). The 1 September flowers item already exists (ua-014).
