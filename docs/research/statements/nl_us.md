# Everyday habits of the Netherlands (NL) and the United States (US): new candidates and sources for existing rows

How the sources were checked. **(F)** means I fetched and read the page. **(S)** means I checked the claim against a search-engine summary of that specific page and did not read the full page. The web-search budget for the session ran out partway through Part B (after about 45 searches), so some US rows have no source. Those rows are marked "none found", which means "not searched to completion". It does not mean the claim is false. Lines marked "Inference" are my own judgement and are not sourced.

## Part A: new candidates (what expats find surprising, and distinctive idioms)

### Takeaway
The best NL material comes from expat humour columns (IamExpat's Invader Stu series), DutchReview's birthday, quirk and untranslatable-word articles, and idiom lists. These yield many insider (obviousness 3) language and humour items. The best US material that "surprises foreigners rather than comes from films" comes from host-family and au-pair guides (ICES, GoAuPair) and from Wikipedia articles on US-born customs: gender reveals, white elephant, Elf on the Shelf, Friendsgiving and spelling bees. US idioms are mostly sports-derived ("ballpark", "Monday morning quarterback", "table it").

## Overlap with the other nine cultures (who else shares each habit)

### Takeaway
Most NL idioms and most US party customs are distinctive among the ten. The main overlaps: kissing, half-a-year phrasing and the "elephant from a fly" idiom with PL and UA; lingering at the table with the Latin and Mediterranean cultures; showers and gender reveals with BR, MX and KR; milk with meals and short parties between NL and US.

### Cited Findings
- Gender reveal parties are reported as growing in South Korea — [Wikipedia: Gender reveal party](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gender_reveal_party)
- Brazil has "chá de bebê", and Italy holds a party around 3–4 months of pregnancy with a gender revelation; Russia and the CIS traditionally have no baby showers — [Wikipedia: Baby shower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Baby_shower)
- Bridal showers are seen in the UK as a "consumerist American idea"; Denmark and Germany only hold bachelor and bachelorette parties — [Wikipedia: Bridal shower](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bridal_shower)

- "What do you do?" reads as intrusive in the Netherlands and France — [fodmapeveryday / MSN list](https://www.fodmapeveryday.com/14-american-habits-and-phrases-that-can-be-considered-rude-abroad/) (S)
- MDY dates and Fahrenheit are not used by any of the other nine — [date formats](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_date_formats_by_country); [Fahrenheit](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fahrenheit)
