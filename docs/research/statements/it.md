# Everyday habits of Italy (IT): candidate statements

Research notes for the Whose Habits statement bank. 123 candidates in total; keep about 50. By topic: daily 28, family 20, hosting 15, humour 12, work 15, language 33. Every source URL below was opened during this research. Items I saw only in search snippets are listed under Gaps, not as candidates.
Obviousness spread: 1 = 8, 2 = 60, 3 = 55, so about 93% are level 2–3.
"Also fits" uses the codes PL UA NL BR MX US TR FR KR. I added cultures generously where I know they share a practice, and that knowledge is my own judgement, not taken from the source (marked "(fits = my judgement)" where it matters most).

## Q1. What do expats find surprising in daily life, family life and humour?

### Takeaway
Expat accounts point most often to bar rituals, health folklore about air and cold (colpo d'aria, cervicale, congestione), superstitions with an Italian twist (17, touching iron), self-service rules in supermarkets, and family milestones with their own rituals: the laurel wreath and papiro at graduation, and the birth ribbon on the door. Humour leans on teasing: rivalry between towns, football banter, the April paper fish, and "colmo" riddles.

### Cited Findings

#### daily

- **Habit:** In many city bars you pay at the till first, then hand the receipt to the barista to get your coffee.
- **Detail:** Pay at the "cassa", take the "scontrino" to the "banco"; the barista tears it. Standard in big-city and tourist bars; small southern bars often let you pay on the way out.
- **Topic:** daily — **Obviousness:** 2
- **Also fits:** none
- **Source:** https://dreamofitaly.com/?p=19714
- **Confidence:** high, but regional (more common in Rome and other cities, less in small towns)

- **Habit:** Coffee at a bar is drunk standing at the counter, and sitting at a table costs more.
- **Detail:** Menus list two prices, "banco" (standing) and "tavolo" (table service). Locals drink standing up in a few minutes.
- **Topic:** daily — **Obviousness:** 2
- **Also fits:** FR (counter and table prices differ in French cafés too; fits = my judgement)
- **Source:** https://dreamofitaly.com/?p=19714
- **Confidence:** high

- **Habit:** At the supermarket you put on a plastic glove before touching fruit, then weigh it and print the price sticker yourself.
- **Detail:** Gloves sit next to the produce. You type the item's number into the "bilancia" and stick on the label; cashiers send back unweighed bags.
- **Topic:** daily — **Obviousness:** 2
- **Also fits:** PL FR (self-weighing is common there; the glove rule is the Italian part; fits = my judgement)
- **Source:** https://expatica.com/it/living/household/italian-supermarkets-79836
- **Confidence:** high

- **Habit:** People dress for the calendar, not the thermometer: no bare legs outside summer, even on a warm day.
- **Detail:** Bare legs are rare outside June to August. Scarves and padded jackets come out by date, not by temperature.

- **Habit:** Going out with wet hair is widely believed to give you a stiff neck or an earache.
- **Detail:** You get a "colpo d'aria" ("hit of air"), named by body part: "all'orecchio" (ear), "al collo" (neck). Wet hair, AC and an open window while sweaty are the usual culprits.

- **Source:** https://www.thelocal.it/20201027/italian-expression-of-the-day-colpo-daria

- **Habit:** A thin undershirt called the "health vest" is the standard defence against catching a chill.

- **Topic:** daily — **Obviousness:** 3

- **Source:** https://www.thelocal.it/20201027/italian-expression-of-the-day-colpo-daria

## Q3. What workplace and hosting etiquette is distinctive?

### Takeaway
Work: titles from your degree (dottore, ingegnere), the formal Lei, a real lunch away from the desk, the coffee break where decisions happen, meal vouchers, the 13th and 14th salary, bridge days, and the August shutdown. Hosting: say "permesso" at the door, bring pastries not a dish, never chrysanthemums, stay well past the coffee, and leave with leftovers. Sources disagree on whether to arrive on time or 10–15 minutes late.
