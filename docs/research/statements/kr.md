# South Korea (KR): everyday habits, phrases and small rituals, candidate statements

Method note: every URL below was opened with WebFetch during this session. Reddit (old.reddit.com) could not be fetched, and the session's web-search budget ran out before the delivery-dish, rock-paper-scissors and host-fruit searches, so those topics are listed under Gaps. Off-limits items were skipped on purpose: plastic surgery, skincare and appearance, soju and hoesik drinking, military service, North Korea, K-pop and kimchi or BBQ as dishes. Borderline items are flagged inline.

There are 98 candidates (target was about 70; trim to about 50). By topic: daily 18, family 26, hosting 13, language 14, humour 12, work 15. By obviousness: 1 = 6, 2 = 52, 3 = 40. The bank is light on level 1, so reviewers may want to bump some obvious ones (shoes off, two hands, oppa, "fighting", bowing) or accept that KR is mostly a level 2–3 culture for this game.

## Key question 1: What do expats find surprising in daily life (daily, family, hosting)?

### Takeaway
Expat surprise lists split into small physical habits (floor 4 shown as "F", cancelling a lift button, teeth brushing after lunch, hazard-light "thank you", no public bins) and a dense calendar of family and couple rituals (100-day baby party, first-birthday item grab, 14th-of-the-month couple days, cash-envelope weddings with meal tickets). Many of these are documented in Korean English-language newspapers with 2023–2026 survey data.

### Cited findings: DAILY

1. **Habit:** In many buildings the lift shows "F" instead of the number for the fourth floor.
   **Detail:** 사 (sa) means both "four" and "death"; flats with several 4s are also said to sell for less.
   **Topic:** daily. **Obviousness:** 2
   **Also fits:** none
   **Source:** https://www.koreabridge.net/post/korean-superstitions-just-may-save-your-life-90daykorean ; https://thesoulofseoul.net/things-surprise-korea/
   **Confidence:** high

2. **Habit:** Pressed the wrong floor in the lift? Press it again and the button switches off.
   **Detail:** Most lifts let you cancel a floor by pressing it a second time (often a double-tap). Expats list it as a small surprise.
   **Topic:** daily. **Obviousness:** 3
   **Also fits:** none (the feature exists elsewhere but isn't widely known)
   **Source:** https://thesoulofseoul.net/things-surprise-korea/
   **Confidence:** medium (it's a lift feature, not a cultural rule)

3. **Habit:** Drivers flash their hazard lights to say "thanks" or "sorry" to the car behind.
   **Detail:** The hazards (bisanggaebangdeung) also signal "I'm stuck", but a quick blink after someone lets you merge means thanks.
   **Topic:** daily. **Obviousness:** 3
   **Also fits:** PL UA TR (hazard-light thanks is common on their roads too)
   **Source:** https://thesoulofseoul.net/things-surprise-korea/
   **Confidence:** high

4. **Habit:** People keep a toothbrush at work or school and brush their teeth after lunch.
   **Detail:** It follows the "3-3-3" rule: three times a day, within three minutes of eating, for three minutes. Office bathrooms are full of people brushing at 1 pm.
   **Topic:** daily. **Obviousness:** 2
   **Also fits:** none
   **Source:** https://studyabroadblog.drexel.edu/2024/12/29/questions-i-asked-my-korean-friends-during-my-first-month-in-korea/ ; https://www.expatguidekorea.com/blog/how-korea-has-changed-my-daily-habits.html
   **Confidence:** high

5. **Habit:** Restaurant tables have a buzzer for calling staff, or diners simply call out "over here!"
   **Detail:** "Yeogiyo!" (여기요, "here!") or the table bell is normal and not rude. Diners don't wait to catch a server's eye.
   **Topic:** daily. **Obviousness:** 2
   **Also fits:** none
   **Source:** https://thesoulofseoul.net/things-surprise-korea/
   **Confidence:** high

6. **Habit:** You can have fried chicken delivered to a spot on the grass in a riverside park.
   **Detail:** Han River picnickers order to "delivery zones" in the park. Delivery has a long history: a diary records lunch being ordered to an office in 1768.
   **Topic:** daily. **Obviousness:** 2
   **Also fits:** none
   **Source:** https://thesoulofseoul.net/things-surprise-korea/ ; https://www.korean-culture.org/eng/webzine/202108/sub01.html
   **Confidence:** high

7. **Habit:** Public bins are rare, so people carry their rubbish home.
   **Detail:** Street bins were removed when strict paid recycling and volume-based bags came in. A few remain in tourist areas.
   **Topic:** daily. **Obviousness:** 2
   **Also fits:** none
   **Source:** https://thesoulofseoul.net/things-surprise-korea/ ; https://studyabroadblog.drexel.edu/2024/12/29/questions-i-asked-my-korean-friends-during-my-first-month-in-korea/
   **Confidence:** high

8. **Habit:** Pupils and some office workers swap their shoes for indoor slippers when they arrive.
   **Detail:** Indoor shoes (silnaehwa) are standard at schools. Restaurants with floor seating also ask you to take your shoes off.
   **Topic:** daily. **Obviousness:** 2
   **Also fits:** PL UA (school "change shoes")
   **Source:** https://thesoulofseoul.net/things-surprise-korea/
   **Confidence:** high

9. **Habit:** Shoes come off at the door, and there are separate slippers just for the bathroom.
   **Detail:** Wet-room bathrooms have their own plastic slippers (yokshil sillipeo), even for a 3 a.m. visit.
   **Topic:** daily. **Obviousness:** 1 (shoes off), 2 (bathroom slippers)
   **Also fits:** PL UA TR (shoes off). Bathroom slippers: TR.
   **Source:** https://www.expatguidekorea.com/blog/how-korea-has-changed-my-daily-habits.html
   **Confidence:** high

10. **Habit:** Holding the door for the person behind you is rare and can feel a bit odd.
    **Detail:** An expat says she stopped doing it because it seemed awkward. People rarely hold doors, even for prams.
    **Topic:** daily. **Obviousness:** 3
    **Also fits:** none
    **Source:** https://www.expatguidekorea.com/blog/how-korea-has-changed-my-daily-habits.html ; https://thesoulofseoul.net/things-surprise-korea/
    **Confidence:** medium (anecdotal, but two independent sources)

11. **Habit:** Electric fans come with sleep timers, and some people warn against a fan running all night in a closed room.
    **Detail:** The "fan death" belief (seonpunggi samangseol) dates to a 1927 newspaper scare. Many people treat the timer as a safety feature.
    **Topic:** daily. **Obviousness:** 2
    **Also fits:** none
    **Source:** https://www.koreabridge.net/post/korean-superstitions-just-may-save-your-life-90daykorean ; https://thesoulofseoul.net/things-surprise-korea/
    **Confidence:** high (fading among young people; it's still debated in expat groups every summer)

12. **Habit:** Waiters cut noodles and grilled meat with kitchen scissors right at the table.
    **Detail:** Gawi (가위, scissors) is a standard table tool, and servers snip long noodles into portions for you.
    **Topic:** daily. **Obviousness:** 2
    **Also fits:** none
    **Source:** https://thesoulofseoul.net/things-surprise-korea/
    **Confidence:** high

13. **Habit:** Instead of tips, a restaurant you visit often brings free extras and calls it "service".
    **Detail:** Seobiseu (서비스) means a freebie such as a drink or an extra dish. Tipping isn't done.
    **Topic:** daily. **Obviousness:** 2
    **Also fits:** TR (free tea or dessert "ikram")
    **Source:** https://thesoulofseoul.net/things-surprise-korea/
    **Confidence:** high

14. **Habit:** People study or do their work for hours in cafés, with headphones on and a laptop open.
    **Detail:** Cagongjok (카공족) is the "café-study tribe": cafe + gongbu (study) + jok (tribe). In 2021, 60% of people in their 20s did it (Hankook Research).
    **Topic:** daily. **Obviousness:** 2
    **Also fits:** US
    **Source:** https://www.koreaherald.com/article/3149697
    **Confidence:** high (2023 article; Starbucks Korea later banned printers and desktops)

15. **Habit:** Taxi drivers find you faster if you name the neighbourhood rather than the street address.
    **Detail:** Old lot-based addresses (by neighbourhood, dong) and new street addresses coexist, and people still navigate by dong names.
    **Topic:** daily. **Obviousness:** 3
    **Also fits:** none
    **Source:** https://thesoulofseoul.net/things-surprise-korea/
    **Confidence:** medium

16. **Habit:** Many people check the fine-dust forecast each morning to decide whether to wear a mask.
    **Detail:** Misemeonji (미세먼지, fine dust) levels are part of the daily weather report, and an app check is routine.
    **Topic:** daily. **Obviousness:** 2
    **Also fits:** none
    **Source:** https://thesoulofseoul.net/things-surprise-korea/
    **Confidence:** high (seasonal: spring is worst)

17. **Habit:** Jiggling your leg under the table is said to shake your luck and money away.
    **Detail:** Legs are linked to fortune, and elders will tell you "don't shake your legs, your luck will run off" (dari tteolji ma).
    **Topic:** daily. **Obviousness:** 3
    **Also fits:** TR (leg-shaking is frowned on too, though for other reasons; add to be safe)
    **Source:** https://www.koreabridge.net/post/korean-superstitions-just-may-save-your-life-90daykorean
    **Confidence:** medium

18. **Habit:** Whistling at night is avoided because it is said to call ghosts and snakes.
    **Detail:** Bam-e hwiparam (밤에 휘파람): it's said to summon gwisin (ghosts) and snakes. Whistle only in daylight.
    **Topic:** daily. **Obviousness:** 3
    **Also fits:** TR UA (both have their own whistling taboos)
    **Source:** https://www.koreabridge.net/post/korean-superstitions-just-may-save-your-life-90daykorean
    **Confidence:** medium

### Cited findings: FAMILY

19. **Habit:** Writing someone's name in red ink is a big no-no.
    **Detail:** The names of the dead were traditionally written in red, so a living person's name in red is a bad omen. Other text in red is fine.
    **Topic:** family. **Obviousness:** 2
    **Also fits:** none
    **Source:** https://www.koreabridge.net/post/korean-superstitions-just-may-save-your-life-90daykorean
    **Confidence:** high (some business guides say it is fading: https://koreaexperience.com/blog/korean-work-culture-office-etiquette-for-foreigners)

20. **Habit:** Families check the calendar for a "spirit-free day" before moving house.
    **Detail:** Son eomneun nal (손없는날) are days with no evil spirits about. Moving companies charge more on them.
    **Topic:** family. **Obviousness:** 3
    **Also fits:** none
    **Source:** https://www.koreabridge.net/post/korean-superstitions-just-may-save-your-life-90daykorean
    **Confidence:** high (the price premium is widely reported, but this source only covers the belief)

21. **Habit:** Shoes are a risky gift for a partner, because they might "run away" in them.
    **Detail:** Shoes (sinbal) are said to make the recipient walk off. This is told most often between couples.
    **Topic:** family. **Obviousness:** 3
    **Also fits:** none
    **Source:** https://www.koreabridge.net/post/korean-superstitions-just-may-save-your-life-90daykorean
    **Confidence:** medium

22. **Habit:** Students get sticky toffee before a big exam so the answers "stick".
    **Detail:** Yeot (엿) is rice taffy; it makes you "stick" to a pass, i.e. get in. Parents tape it to school gates before the national exam.
    **Topic:** family. **Obviousness:** 2
    **Also fits:** none
    **Source:** https://www.koreabridge.net/post/korean-superstitions-just-may-save-your-life-90daykorean
    **Confidence:** high

24. **Habit:** Mum, dad or grandma has a dream that "announced" a baby, and the family retells it for years.

## Key question 2: Which phrases, address terms and expressions are distinctive (language, humour)?

### Takeaway
Language is the richest seam. Age-based address terms (oppa, eonni, hyeong, nuna, sunbae) are used for non-relatives, uri ("our") replaces "my", and food greetings are everywhere. Humour runs on puns (ajae gag), personality-type banter (MBTI, blood type) and generational teasing (kkondae, "latte is horse").

## Key question 3: What workplace and hosting etiquette is distinctive (work)?

### Takeaway
Work life is organised around titles plus "-nim" instead of names, leaving after the boss, seniority by entry year, set phrases at the end of the day, compulsory-feeling team lunches and messenger-app work chat. Some customs are being phased out, notably juniors treating the boss and part of the hoesik culture.

### Cited findings: WORK

84. **Habit:** At work you call your manager by job title plus an honorific, never by first name.
    **Detail:** Gwajang-nim (과장님, section chief) or Kim daeri-nim. Juniors get name + nim or ssi.
    **Topic:** work. **Obviousness:** 2
    **Also fits:** none

    **Topic:** work. **Obviousness:** 2
