# France (FR): everyday habits, phrases and small rituals

Research for the statement bank. 72 candidates below. Every "Source" URL was opened with a fetch tool unless the entry is marked **[excerpt only]**: those were seen only as a search-result excerpt, so check them before using the entry. Off-limits topics were avoided (no strikes, politics, religion, wine clichés or dish clichés). Where an entry touches a borderline area (wine, clothing, a food ritual), it is flagged.

Obviousness mix: 1 = 13, 2 = 40, 3 = 19 (about 82% are level 2–3).

## What do expats find surprising, which idioms are distinctive, and what is the hosting and workplace etiquette? (KQ1–3)

### Takeaway
The richest material is the greeting ritual (bonjour as a requirement in shops, lifts, waiting rooms and offices), meal and table rules, calendar rituals (galette, paper fish, lily of the valley, neighbours' day, New Year wishes all through January), ironic "second-degree" humour with complaining as small talk, and a large stock of food and animal idioms. The workplace is defined by greetings, sociable lunches, the office "pot" and formality (vous, hierarchy).

#### DAILY

- **Habit:** Walking into a small shop without saying "good morning" to the staff is rude.
- **Detail:** "Bonjour" before 6 pm, "bonsoir" after, and "merci, au revoir" on the way out. Skipping it gets you cold service.
- **Topic:** daily — **Obviousness:** 1
- **Also fits:** IT PL UA MX
- **Source:** https://www.learnfrenchwithclemence.com/blog/10-everyday-things-french-people-do-that-surprise-americans

- **Habit:** To get a shop assistant's attention, you start with "hello", because "excuse me" doesn't count as a greeting.
- **Detail:** Open with "bonjour", not "excusez-moi", even when you only have a quick question.
- **Topic:** daily — **Obviousness:** 2
- **Also fits:** none
- **Source:** https://www.thelocal.fr/20220415/explained-when-should-you-greet-a-french-person
- **Confidence:** high

- **Habit:** Walking into a doctor's waiting room, you say hello to all the strangers already waiting.

#### HOSTING

- **Habit:** If dinner is at 8, polite guests arrive at about 8:15, never early.
- **Detail:** "Le quart d'heure de politesse" gives the host time to finish. More than about 20 minutes late, you should text.
- **Topic:** hosting — **Obviousness:** 2
- **Also fits:** BR MX TR UA
- **Source:** https://www.thelocal.fr/20240109/no-beer-pong-9-things-to-know-before-you-go-to-a-french-party
- **Confidence:** high. Not every French person expects it. Restaurant bookings are the exception.

- **Habit:** Some regions even have their own name for the polite 15 minutes late.
- **Detail:** In the Charente it's "le quart d'heure charentais".
- **Topic:** hosting — **Obviousness:** 3
- **Also fits:** none
- **Source:** https://frenchentree.com/living-in-france/avoid-these-french-food-faux-pas
- **Confidence:** medium. Regional (Charente, south-west).

- **Habit:** At a party you go round and greet every guest individually, with cheek kisses, even ones you don't know.
- **Detail:** "Faire la bise" to everyone. A wave from the door isn't enough, and you introduce yourself to strangers.
- **Topic:** hosting — **Obviousness:** 1
- **Also fits:** BR MX IT NL TR
- **Source:** https://www.thelocal.fr/20240109/no-beer-pong-9-things-to-know-before-you-go-to-a-french-party
- **Confidence:** high. Since COVID, the bise is more selective.

- **Habit:** Leaving a party, you go round and say goodbye to everyone again, kisses and all.
- **Detail:** "Bonne soirée" plus a bise to each person. Slipping out unnoticed is frowned on.
- **Topic:** hosting — **Obviousness:** 2
- **Also fits:** BR MX IT NL
- **Source:** https://www.thelocal.fr/20251017/seven-ways-to-make-a-great-impression-at-a-french-home

- **Habit:** For pre-dinner drinks you bring something salty to nibble, not something sweet.

- **Habit:** When clinking glasses, you must look each person in the eye, or it's bad luck.

- **Habit:** Bread goes straight on the tablecloth next to your plate, not on a side plate.

#### FAMILY

- **Habit:** When the January cake is cut, the youngest child hides under the table and says who gets each slice.
- **Detail:** "Galette des rois": the cutter asks "pour qui?" and the child names a guest at random, so nobody can cheat.

- **Also fits:** none

- **Habit:** Whoever finds the tiny figurine hidden in the January cake wears a paper crown for the day.
- **Detail:** The "fève" was once a bean, now a porcelain trinket. Galettes are eaten all through January, not just on 6 January.
- **Topic:** family — **Obviousness:** 2

- **Source:** https://languatalk.com/blog/podcast/french/la-tradition-de-la-galette-des-rois/
- **Confidence:** high. **Flag:** cake, but it's a ritual, not a dish cliché.

- **Habit:** Colleagues share the crown cake at the office throughout January.

- **Confidence:** medium

#### HUMOUR

## Which of the other nine cultures share each habit? (KQ4)
