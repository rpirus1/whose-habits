# Everyday habits of Türkiye: candidate statements

Notes for the report writer:
- Every entry below cites a page that was actually opened (WebFetch) during this research, except where the entry says "search snippet only". Those few are flagged and should be re-checked before use.
- The "Also fits" codes are my own judgement, made generously as CONTENT_RULES asks ("when in doubt, add the culture"). They are not taken from the sources.
- Things deliberately left out: anything religious (Ramadan, mosque etiquette, inşallah/maşallah as religious formulas, circumcision), Atatürk, Kurdish or Armenian topics, politics, alcohol, food clichés (kebab, baklava as a "national food" item), landmarks and flags (including the wedding flag-raising custom).
- Borderline items are flagged inline: holiday-linked (Bayram) customs and phrases with religious etymology.
- Count: about 85 candidates. By topic: daily 14, hosting 12, family 15, humour 9, work 10, language 15 full entries plus 10 compact backups.
- Format: daily, humour and the first 15 language items use the full field layout. Hosting, work and family use a nested-bullet version with the same fields. The language backups are one-liners: topic, obviousness, fits, URL, confidence.

## What do expats and visitors find surprising in daily life?

### Takeaway
Expats mostly notice the warm-but-intrusive sociability (personal questions, instant invitations, "geliyorum" timekeeping), small protective superstitions (nazar, knock-on-wood with earlobe pull, handbag off the floor), and street-level routines such as the çaycı tea tray, the basket on a rope and street-cat feeding.

### Cited Findings

#### daily

- **Habit:** A blue eye-shaped glass charm hangs in cars and doorways, and a cracked one gets replaced right away.
- **Detail:** Nazar boncuğu, against envious looks. If it cracks it has "done its job" absorbing the evil eye and is swapped for a new one.
- **Topic:** daily — **Obviousness:** 1
- **Also fits:** none
- **Source:** https://fethiyetimes.com/?p=3689
- **Confidence:** high

- **Habit:** People in flats lower a basket on a rope so the corner shop can put their groceries in it.
- **Detail:** Sepet. The resident phones the bakkal (corner shop), a clerk fills the basket and takes the money from it. An old-Istanbul habit.
- **Topic:** daily — **Obviousness:** 3
- **Also fits:** IT (Naples has the same "panaro" basket)
- **Source:** https://www.vagabondjourney.com/basket-lift-in-turkey/
- **Confidence:** medium. The source is a 2009 eyewitness account. Fading, and mostly older Istanbul neighbourhoods.

- **Habit:** Shopkeepers and neighbours put out water bowls and food for the street cats on their block.
- **Detail:** The care is informal: one shop feeds a cat and another gives it water, and makeshift shelters go up in winter. City vets help with sterilising.
- **Topic:** daily — **Obviousness:** 2
- **Also fits:** none (Rome has cat-feeders, but it's far less of an everyday street norm)
- **Source:** https://istanbul.com/blog/local-authentic/cats-of-istanbul
- **Confidence:** high. Strongest in Istanbul.

- **Habit:** People crack salted sunflower seeds with their teeth while chatting or watching a match, leaving piles of shells.
- **Detail:** Çekirdek is the most-eaten dried snack. Üsküdar (Istanbul) banned eating it on its shoreline in 2020 because of the shell litter.
- **Topic:** daily — **Obviousness:** 2
- **Also fits:** UA (semechky habit is near-identical)
- **Source:** https://hurriyetdailynews.com/eating-sunflower-seeds-banned-on-an-istanbul-coast-156423
- **Confidence:** high

- **Habit:** A handbag never goes on the floor, because then the money walks out the door.
- **Detail:** Folk belief that a purse on the floor loses money. Special handbag hooks for café tables are sold for this.
- **Topic:** daily — **Obviousness:** 3
- **Also fits:** BR (same "bolsa no chão" belief)
- **Source:** https://fethiyetimes.com/?p=4219
- **Confidence:** medium

- **Habit:** Hearing about someone's bad luck, people pull an earlobe and knock twice on wood.
- **Detail:** Wards off the same misfortune. The earlobe tug plus the double knock is the local twist on knocking on wood.
- **Topic:** daily — **Obviousness:** 2
- **Also fits:** none for the earlobe version (knocking on wood alone would fit PL UA US NL BR)
- **Source:** https://fethiyetimes.com/?p=3689
- **Confidence:** high. Sources differ on whether it's one ear or both, and how many knocks (see Wikipedia below).

- **Habit:** Cutting your nails after dark is avoided because it brings bad luck.
- **Detail:** Folk belief that it brings poverty or worse to the family.
- **Topic:** daily — **Obviousness:** 3
- **Also fits:** KR (the same night-nail taboo exists)
- **Source:** https://fethiyetimes.com/?p=3689
- **Confidence:** medium. Older generations, fading.

- **Habit:** Chewing gum at night is frowned upon by grandparents.
- **Detail:** Folk belief that chewing gum after dark is "eating the flesh of the dead".
- **Topic:** daily — **Obviousness:** 3
- **Also fits:** none
- **Source:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superstition_in_Turkey
- **Confidence:** medium. Wikipedia only, and fading.

- **Habit:** A slipper left upside down gets flipped back over at once.
- **Detail:** Folk belief that upside-down slippers in the house foretell a death.
- **Topic:** daily — **Obviousness:** 3
- **Also fits:** none
- **Source:** https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Superstition_in_Turkey
- **Confidence:** medium. Wikipedia only.

- **Habit:** Stale bread is never binned: it's left on a wall or a ledge for the birds instead.
- **Detail:** Bread (ekmek) is treated as almost sacred, and throwing it away is believed to bring bad luck.
- **Topic:** daily — **Obviousness:** 2
- **Also fits:** UA PL (strong bread-respect norms)
- **Source:** https://fethiyetimes.com/?p=3689
- **Confidence:** high

- **Habit:** A bottle of lemon cologne is used to clean hands before and after meals.
- **Detail:** Kolonya works as hand sanitiser, aftershave and even a bite remedy. Its role as a sanitiser surged in 2020.
- **Topic:** daily — **Obviousness:** 2
- **Also fits:** none
- **Source:** https://fethiyetimes.com/?p=4219
- **Confidence:** high

- **Habit:** Cold drafts are blamed for almost every illness, so babies get bundled up even in summer.
- **Detail:** Cereyan (draft) fear. Cold floors are also blamed for women's health problems, so slippers are a must.
- **Topic:** daily — **Obviousness:** 2
- **Also fits:** PL UA IT KR (all have strong draft/fan-death beliefs)
- **Source:** https://gadling.com/?p=29778
- **Confidence:** medium. A single expat blog, plus the search result for an Adana study on cold-related folk beliefs.

- **Habit:** At a bus door people bunch into a crowd rather than forming a line.
- **Detail:** One expat guide puts it as "Turks don't queue, they bunch", with a surge when the door opens.
- **Topic:** daily — **Obviousness:** 2
- **Also fits:** UA IT
- **Source:** https://www.propertyturkey.com/blog-turkey/9-turkish-habits-that-will-drive-you-crazy-and-how-to-manage-them
- **Confidence:** medium. Could read as a negative stereotype, so word it neutrally.

- **Habit:** A shopkeeper brushes their chin or goods with the first banknote of the day for luck.
- **Detail:** The first sale (siftah) is "blessed" so that more sales follow. Some scatter the note on the ground.
- **Topic:** daily — **Obviousness:** 3
- **Also fits:** none
- **Source:** https://www.insideoutinistanbul.com/body-language/
- **Confidence:** medium. Bazaar and market traders; the word "siftah" is my addition and is not in the source.

#### humour (humour and small talk)

- **Habit:** A taxi driver may ask your salary, marital status and rent within five minutes.
- **Detail:** Personal questions are a friendly way of placing you. One expat deflects with "bu özel" ("that's private").
- **Topic:** humour — **Obviousness:** 2
- **Also fits:** KR (age and marriage questions), UA
- **Source:** https://www.propertyturkey.com/blog-turkey/culture-shock-expats-reveal-the-turkey-no-one-tells-you-about
- **Confidence:** high

- **Habit:** "I'm on my way!" can mean the person is still at home in pyjamas.
- **Detail:** "Geliyorum" ("I'm coming"). Expect friends to be late and tradespeople to come another day.
- **Topic:** humour — **Obviousness:** 2
- **Also fits:** MX BR (cf. "ahorita")
- **Source:** https://www.propertyturkey.com/blog-turkey/9-turkish-habits-that-will-drive-you-crazy-and-how-to-manage-them
- **Confidence:** high

- **Habit:** If you put yourself down, people instantly protest "Not at all!" instead of laughing along.
- **Detail:** "Estağfurullah" is used to reject self-deprecation. Self-mockery jokes tend not to land.
- **Topic:** humour — **Obviousness:** 3
- **Also fits:** none
- **Source:** https://www.dailysabah.com/life/communication-breakdown-conundrums-between-turks-and-expats/news/amp
- **Confidence:** medium. Opinion column. BORDERLINE: the word has an Arabic religious etymology but is used as a set politeness phrase. Keep the word in the note only.

- **Habit:** Suggest meeting next week and the reply is often "Why not right now?"
- **Detail:** A spontaneous planning style. Plans made far ahead are soft, so people reconfirm bookings close to the date.
- **Topic:** humour — **Obviousness:** 3
- **Also fits:** BR
- **Source:** https://www.dailysabah.com/life/communication-breakdown-conundrums-between-turks-and-expats/news/amp
- **Confidence:** medium. Single author's experience.

- **Habit:** Ask for help and you get a cheerful "We'll do it!" without a word about when.
- **Detail:** "Yaparız" signals genuine willingness, but not a timeline.
- **Topic:** humour — **Obviousness:** 3
- **Also fits:** MX BR
- **Source:** https://www.dailysabah.com/life/communication-breakdown-conundrums-between-turks-and-expats/news/amp
- **Confidence:** medium

- **Habit:** A chat with a stranger on a ferry can move to lost parents and old loves within minutes.
- **Detail:** People like sharing their lives and asking about yours. Taxi, ferry and queue conversations often get deep.
- **Topic:** humour — **Obviousness:** 2
- **Also fits:** BR UA
- **Source:** https://www.dailysabah.com/life/communication-tips-for-talking-with-turks/news/amp
- **Confidence:** medium

- **Habit:** Punchlines from a centuries-old trickster's anecdotes are dropped into everyday arguments.
- **Detail:** Nasreddin Hoca tales, e.g. "the quilt is gone, the fight is over" (a quarrel that was really about taking your stuff).
- **Topic:** humour — **Obviousness:** 3
- **Also fits:** none
- **Source:** https://cappadociahistory.com/post/nasreddin-hoca
- **Confidence:** medium. The source confirms the sayings are quoted in daily life but doesn't explain them; the gloss is mine.

- **Habit:** Newcomers learn that sarcastic banter often falls flat; it's used less than in English.
- **Detail:** An American columnist's observation. Turks may take sarcasm literally, so warmth reads better than irony.
- **Topic:** humour — **Obviousness:** 3
- **Also fits:** KR
- **Source:** https://www.dailysabah.com/life/communication-breakdown-conundrums-between-turks-and-expats/news/amp
- **Confidence:** low. Opinion piece; risky as a generalisation.

- **Habit:** Soap-opera plots are standard small talk in cafés, hair salons and on buses.
- **Detail:** Dizi are on screens everywhere; one expat guide suggests watching them to have something to talk about.
- **Topic:** humour — **Obviousness:** 1
- **Also fits:** BR MX KR
- **Source:** https://www.propertyturkey.com/blog-turkey/9-turkish-habits-that-will-drive-you-crazy-and-how-to-manage-them
- **Confidence:** medium. Low game value, because it fits many cultures.

### Inferences
- The richest "insider" seam is folk superstition (handbag, slippers, nails, gum, earlobe). Several are fading among urban young people, so they suit obviousness 3 but need a "grandparents" framing.
- Humour is the thinnest topic: sources describe small-talk style better than jokes. Only 9 candidates, and some are weak (sarcasm, soap operas).

### Gaps
- Dolmuş fares passed hand to hand to the driver: the only evidence is search snippets (https://www.theworldswaiting.com/2011/08/unravelling-turkish-dolmus.html). I didn't open it; good candidate (obviousness 2, also fits UA marshrutka).
- "Hocam" ("my teacher") used for anyone, colleagues included: only a search snippet (https://elon.io/grammar/turkish/pragmatics/address-terms). Not opened.
- Honking as communication: only snippets (dutchreview). Not opened.
- Ears ringing = someone talking about you (right ear good, left bad): snippet from a Property Turkey superstitions page, not opened.
- "Kalk git kahvesi" (a coffee that signals guests should leave): snippet only, and seems regional or historical.
- No Reddit thread could be fetched; r/Turkey and r/istanbul are not represented.
- No good source found on football-rivalry banter in families or workplaces, or on Temel jokes. Temel jokes were also deliberately avoided, since they target a regional or ethnic group.

## Which idioms and everyday phrases are distinctive and translate funnily?

### Takeaway
Turkish has a dense set of situational formulas: wishes for health after a meal, a haircut or a sneeze, and for anyone at work. These make great obviousness 2–3 language items. There are also many vivid food and animal idioms (zucchini, parsley, flea-camel, goats).

### Cited Findings

#### language

- **Habit:** After a home-cooked meal, guests wish "health to your hands" to the cook.
- **Detail:** "Eline sağlık" / "Ellerinize sağlık". The cook replies "afiyet olsun". It also thanks anyone for handmade work or help.
- **Topic:** language — **Obviousness:** 2
- **Also fits:** none
- **Source:** https://yabangee.com/23-turkish-phrases-wish-someone-taught/
- **Confidence:** high

- **Habit:** After someone's haircut or shower, you wish them "may it be healthy".
- **Detail:** "Sıhhatler olsun". Mainly said to men after the barber; the barber says it as you leave the chair.
- **Topic:** language — **Obviousness:** 3
- **Also fits:** none
- **Source:** https://yabangee.com/23-turkish-phrases-wish-someone-taught/
- **Confidence:** high

- **Habit:** Someone sneezes, you say "live long", and they answer "may you live to see it too".
- **Detail:** "Çok yaşa" gets the reply "sen de gör". The reply is the distinctive part.
- **Topic:** language — **Obviousness:** 3
- **Also fits:** none (PL UA have sneeze wishes, but not this reply)
- **Source:** https://yabangee.com/23-turkish-phrases-wish-someone-taught/
- **Confidence:** high

- **Habit:** When you're welcomed in, the fixed reply is "we found it nice".
- **Detail:** "Hoş geldin" ("good that you came") is always answered with "hoş bulduk".
- **Topic:** language — **Obviousness:** 2
- **Also fits:** none
- **Source:** https://yabangee.com/23-turkish-phrases-wish-someone-taught/
- **Confidence:** high

- **Habit:** Strangers slightly older than you are called "big brother" or "big sister".
- **Detail:** Abi/abla ("Ali abi"). Very old people are teyze (auntie) or amca (uncle), friends' parents included.
- **Topic:** language — **Obviousness:** 2
- **Also fits:** KR (oppa/eonni, ajumma), UA (tіtka/dyadko for strangers)
- **Source:** https://yabangee.com/23-turkish-phrases-wish-someone-taught/
- **Confidence:** high

- **Habit:** You say "may it pass" for anything from flu to a cracked phone screen.
- **Detail:** "Geçmiş olsun", a get-well wish for any illness or hardship.
- **Topic:** language — **Obviousness:** 2
- **Also fits:** none
- **Source:** https://yabangee.com/23-turkish-phrases-wish-someone-taught/
- **Confidence:** high. The "cracked phone" example is mine; the source says "any unpleasant situation".

- **Habit:** "Don't be parsley" tells someone to stop meddling.
- **Detail:** "Maydanoz olma". Parsley ends up in every dish, like a busybody in every conversation.
- **Topic:** language — **Obviousness:** 3
- **Also fits:** none
- **Source:** https://storylearning.com/learn/turkish/turkish-tips/turkish-idioms
- **Confidence:** high

- **Habit:** When something gets boring, it "starts to taste like zucchini".
- **Detail:** "Kabak tadı vermek". Courgette as the epitome of blandness.
- **Topic:** language — **Obviousness:** 3
- **Also fits:** none
- **Source:** https://lingopie.com/blog/turkish-idioms/
- **Confidence:** high

- **Habit:** Blowing something out of proportion is "turning a flea into a camel".
- **Detail:** "Pireyi deve yapmak", the local version of "making a mountain out of a molehill".
- **Topic:** language — **Obviousness:** 3
- **Also fits:** none
- **Source:** https://lingopie.com/blog/turkish-idioms/
- **Confidence:** high

- **Habit:** A nitpicker "splits a hair into forty".
- **Detail:** "Kılı kırk yarmak". Forty is a recurring "many" number.
- **Topic:** language — **Obviousness:** 3
- **Also fits:** PL FR IT (all "split a hair into four")
- **Source:** https://lingopie.com/blog/turkish-idioms/
- **Confidence:** high. Keep the "forty" in the statement to separate it from the others.

- **Habit:** Tired of repeating yourself? "Feathers have grown on my tongue."

- **Topic:** language — **Obviousness:** 3
- **Also fits:** none
- **Source:** https://lingopie.com/blog/turkish-idioms/
- **Confidence:** high

- **Habit:** Someone who loses their mind has "let the goats escape".

- **Topic:** language — **Obviousness:** 3
- **Also fits:** none
- **Source:** https://lingopie.com/blog/turkish-idioms/
- **Confidence:** high

- **Habit:** A blabbermouth is someone in whose mouth "a fava bean won't even get wet".
- **Detail:** "Ağzında bakla ıslanmamak". They spill a secret before it can soak.
- **Topic:** language — **Obviousness:** 3
- **Also fits:** none
- **Source:** https://storylearning.com/learn/turkish/turkish-tips/turkish-idioms
- **Confidence:** high

- **Habit:** A first child, or a first love, is called "the first ache of my eye".

- **Confidence:** high

- **Topic:** language — **Obviousness:** 3
- **Also fits:** none
- **Source:** https://lingopie.com/blog/turkish-idioms/
- **Confidence:** high

### Inferences
- The "health to your hands / mouth / haircut" family is the most distinctive language seam. It's unlikely to be confused with the other 9 cultures, so it's good for any game.
- Avoid "fıstık gibi" ("like a pistachio" = gorgeous), because it's about appearance, and "spit down it's a beard, spit up it's a moustache", which is about bodies. Both are documented at https://lingopie.com/blog/turkish-idioms/ but sit near the off-limits line.

### Gaps
- No source fetched on the everyday use of "kolay gelsin" beyond Yabangee (used in the work section below).
- Didn't verify slang like "oha" in depth; one source (Daily Sabah column) says it can sound rude, so leave it out.

## What workplace and hosting etiquette is distinctive?

### Takeaway
Tea is the backbone of both hosting and work: meetings open with a glass, refusing is unfriendly, and a çaycı brings it. At home: slippers, kolonya, triple offers, returning dishes full, and big compliments to the cook.

### Cited Findings

#### hosting

- **Guests are offered lemon cologne to splash on their hands when they arrive.**
  - Detail: Kolonya, offered at the door or after sweets on holiday visits. A bottle is also a welcome gift.
  - Topic: hosting. Obviousness: 2. Also fits: none.
  - Source: https://www.dailysabah.com/life/the-feast-of-sweets-is-upon-us-what-to-know/news/amp, plus https://fethiyetimes.com/?p=4219
  - Confidence: high. The "on arrival" detail is from the search summary of https://yabangee.com/9-tips-to-know-before-visiting-a-turkish-home/ and of hospitality guides; the fetched Fethiye Times page confirms widespread use for handwashing.
- **Hosts keep spare slippers in a basket by the door for every guest.**
  - Detail: Terlik. Shoes come off at the door, and guest slippers are often too small.
  - Topic: hosting. Obviousness: 1. Also fits: PL UA KR.
  - Source: https://yabangee.com/9-tips-to-know-before-visiting-a-turkish-home/
  - Confidence: high
- **Laying your teaspoon across the top of the glass means "no more tea, thanks".**
  - Detail: Otherwise your tulip glass gets refilled indefinitely.
  - Topic: hosting. Obviousness: 3. Also fits: none.
  - Source: https://fethiyetimes.com/?p=4219
  - Confidence: high
- **Food is offered three times, because the first "no thanks" is assumed to be politeness.**
  - Detail: Many were raised to accept only the third offer; patting your stomach shows you're truly full.
  - Topic: hosting. Obviousness: 2. Also fits: UA PL KR.
  - Source: https://www.dailysabah.com/life/communication-tips-for-talking-with-turks/news/amp
  - Confidence: high
- **A neighbour's dish of food is returned with something homemade on it, never empty.**
  - Detail: Returning a plate empty is impolite; you refill it with your own cooking.
  - Topic: hosting. Obviousness: 2. Also fits: UA KR PL.
  - Source: https://fethiyetimes.com/?p=4238
  - Confidence: high
- **Mention a dish you like and the host may cook it the next time you visit.**
  - Detail: Guests are warned to praise foods carefully, since second helpings will be insisted on.
  - Topic: hosting. Obviousness: 2. Also fits: UA PL IT MX.
  - Source: https://yabangee.com/9-tips-to-know-before-visiting-a-turkish-home/
  - Confidence: medium
- **Visitors greet the eldest person in the room first, then work down by age.**
  - Detail: Grandparents first, sometimes with a hand kiss (see family).
  - Topic: hosting. Obviousness: 2. Also fits: KR.
  - Source: https://yabangee.com/9-tips-to-know-before-visiting-a-turkish-home/
  - Confidence: high
- **The one who invites you to dinner pays the whole bill; splitting is rare.**
  - Detail: You "pay back" by inviting them next time.
  - Topic: hosting. Obviousness: 2. Also fits: KR MX.
  - Source: https://matadornetwork.com/read/11-turkish-customs-know-visiting
  - Confidence: high
- **Sharing a coffee is said to create forty years of friendship.**
  - Detail: Proverb: "Bir fincan kahvenin kırk yıl hatırı vardır." Forty is the go-to "lucky" number.
  - Topic: hosting. Obviousness: 2. Also fits: none.
  - Source: https://fethiyetimes.com/?p=3689 (also https://www.dailysabah.com/life/communication-tips-for-talking-with-turks/news/amp)
  - Confidence: high. The Turkish proverb wording is mine; both sources give it only in English.
- **After coffee, the cup is turned upside down so a friend can read your fortune in the grounds.**
  - Detail: Fal. The saying goes "don't believe fortune-telling, but don't be without it."
  - Topic: hosting. Obviousness: 2. Also fits: none.
  - Source: https://fethiyetimes.com/?p=3689
  - Confidence: high
- **Wearing socks on a visit matters, even in summer; bare feet can offend.**
  - Detail: Expat tip; hosts provide slippers anyway.
  - Topic: hosting. Obviousness: 3. Also fits: none.
  - Source: https://yabangee.com/9-tips-to-know-before-visiting-a-turkish-home/
  - Confidence: medium. Single source.
- **People you just met may invite you to their home, or even their son's wedding.**
  - Detail: A UK expat got a wedding invite from a stranger's father. Fast invitations are normal hospitality.
  - Topic: hosting. Obviousness: 2. Also fits: MX BR.
  - Source: https://www.propertyturkey.com/blog-turkey/culture-shock-expats-reveal-the-turkey-no-one-tells-you-about
  - Confidence: medium

#### work

### Takeaway
I filled the "Also fits" codes per entry using my own knowledge, generously. Roughly 50 of the 74 candidates came out as "none", which makes them usable in any Türkiye game. The strongest overlaps are with UA (seeds, bread, drafts, slippers, returning dishes), KR (elders, hierarchy, holiday money, nail taboo) and MX/BR (time flexibility, "I'm on my way").

### Cited Findings
- The overlap codes are judgement calls, not sourced. ### Inferences
- Items I suggest dropping if trimming to ~50, because they have heavy overlap or low distinctiveness: soap operas, red underwear, bargaining, children in restaurants, queue bunching, sarcasm.
- Items whose overlap is easy to miss: splitting hairs (PL FR IT share it with "four" instead of "forty"), the night-nail taboo (KR), the handbag on the floor (BR), the basket from the window (IT, Naples).

### Gaps
- Overlaps for KR and FR are where I'm least sure; a native reviewer should check the fits for KR on the nail and drafts items, and for MX on the wedding money items.
