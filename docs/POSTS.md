## 1. LinkedIn: first public announcement (2026-10-26)

Attachment: a screen recording of a round.

> Recently my day-to-day communication bubble has expanded to people from all around the world. We talk about plenty of things, and one that always comes up is our cultures and habits. I’d been thinking about building something with Claude Code for a while, so here’s the first result (in beta): https://www.whosehabits.com/
>
> It’s a fun and simple party game for international friends and anyone curious about other countries’ habits. Every player gets an everyday habit on their phone and guesses which country it’s most associated with. 2–12 players, free, no app, no ads, no tracking scripts. Just anonymous game analytics and a Report button, so I can make it better with each iteration.
>
> If you play it with your friends this week, tell me one thing that confused you. I’d be especially thrilled to hear from groups of 5+ people. Don’t agree with a habit? Report it, no hard feelings ;)
>
> A few first reflections on my journey with vibe-coding:
>
> - I’m used to a fast pace at work and I’m fine with it, but this is another level, so it feels a bit unreal
> - the amount of decision-making is still huge (I deliberately prompted the agents to check every plan with me, so I’d learn more)
> - it’s hard to call it a day and take a break: as a PM, I felt I had to keep the agents busy all the time
> - it’s crucial to start validating and getting feedback from the very beginning, otherwise you get carried away with your idea and build just for yourself (which is still quite fun)
>
> Have you started a similar journey? How’s it going?

Results (day 1): 259 impressions, 29 members reached (95% in-network), 0 reactions, 1 comment.

Learnings:

- Reach, not just content: only 29 of 538 followers saw it. Autoplaying video counts repeat views, so 259 impressions overstates it. With no reactions in the first hour, LinkedIn stopped showing it to more people.
- Vertical video turns the post into a full-screen card and the text into a 2-line caption. The first line ("Recently my day-to-day…") didn’t say what this is.
- The ask took too much effort (play a game with 5+ friends, then comment). Nothing could be done in 5 seconds.
- Two stories in one post: a game for friends and AI-building reflections for PMs. Each audience got half a post.
- Next time: one story per post; a 4:5 image or text-only; a question people can answer in a comment in 5 seconds; link in the first comment; ask 5–10 friends directly to look in the first hour; reply to every comment.
